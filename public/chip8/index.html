This isn’t a video or a recreation of a game. It’s the C++ interpreter running the original instructions, right in your browser.

What am I looking at? +

CHIP-8 is a virtual machine introduced in 1977. It has 4 KB of memory, sixteen 8-bit registers and a 64-by-32-pixel screen. These six programs are original ROMs I wrote in CHIP-8 assembly.

What does “Inspect machine” do? +

It opens the real debugger: registers, memory, keypad state and a live disassembly. Pause, then press Step to follow one instruction at a time. Highlighted registers have just changed.

Keyboard field guide +
Space
Pause or resume
H
Open or close the debugger
N
Step one instruction while paused
Backspace
Restart the current ROM
[ / ]
Lower or raise the clock speed
F1–F5
Toggle historical interpreter quirks

Game keys follow a 4 × 4 QWERTY block: 1234, QWER, ASDF, ZXCV. Use the 16-key pad above to see each mapping.

Can I load another game? +

Use “Load your own” to open a local .ch8, .c8 or .rom file. The file stays in your browser. Classic CHIP-8 programs up to 3,584 bytes are supported; Super-CHIP and XO-CHIP extensions are not.
